Anne E. Simon, PhD

Professor, Cell Biology and Molecular Genetics

Program founder; led the Virology Program for 18 years

About

Anne Simon studies RNA viruses of plants, especially umbravirus-like viruses that lack conventional movement proteins. She spent 13 years on the faculty of the University of Massachusetts before joining Maryland in 2000, and co-founded Silvec Biologics, which develops RNA viruses as vectors for plant therapeutics.

Research

Her lab asks how RNA structures control replication and translation in small plus-strand RNA viruses, two processes that cannot happen at the same time on one genome. Work on Turnip crinkle virus, Saguaro cactus virus and Pea enation mosaic virus has revealed cap-independent translation enhancers, including a 3′ enhancer that overlaps a tRNA-like structure and binds 60S ribosomal subunits. The group also studies how RNA folding and polymerase binding switch a genome from translation to replication, and how these viruses make plants more susceptible to other pathogens. An independent mobile RNA from the lab is being developed to deliver treatments into citrus phloem against citrus greening.
