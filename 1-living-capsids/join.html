Become a Virology Program trainee.

Graduate students join through a University of Maryland PhD program and train with Virology Program faculty across four institutions.

How it works

Four steps to joining

  1. Apply to a graduate program

    Apply to a life-sciences PhD program at the University of Maryland, College Park, through the Graduate School.

  2. Rotate through laboratories

    During the first year, complete rotations in Virology Program laboratories at UMD, NIH, Vet Med and IBBR to identify the best fit.

  3. Choose a mentor

    Select a Virology Program faculty member as dissertation advisor, with co-mentors from other partner institutions.

  4. Train with the program

    Complete virology and immunology coursework, attend the monthly group meetings and present at the annual retreat.

Questions

Frequently asked

Application deadlines and funding arrangements change from year to year. Please contact the co-directors for current details.

Who can join?

Highly motivated PhD students with an interest in virology. The program has special outreach to students from minority groups.

Can my mentor be at NIH?

Yes. Program faculty at the National Institutes of Health in Bethesda and Frederick mentor trainees alongside faculty at College Park, Vet Med and IBBR.

What is required of trainees?

Pre-doctoral trainees must attend the monthly group meetings. Trainees also complete program courses and participate in the annual retreat.

Which courses will I take?

Core graduate courses in cell biology, genetics, nucleic acids, bioinformatics and bioethics, plus program courses in molecular virology, viral pathogenesis, vectors and immunology.

Who do I contact?

Email the program co-directors, Jeffrey DeStefano (jdestefa@umd.edu) and Eric Freed (efreed@mail.nih.gov), for current admissions details.

Study virology with us.

Questions about rotations, mentoring or admissions may be directed to the program co-directors, Jeffrey DeStefano (UMD) and Eric Freed (NIH).
