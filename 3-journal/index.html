Graduate training in virology across four institutions.

Specialized PhD training in host-virus interactions and in viral immunology and vaccine design. Trainees work with faculty mentors at the University of Maryland, the National Institutes of Health, the Virginia-Maryland College of Veterinary Medicine and the Institute for Bioscience and Biotechnology Research.

The program brings together human, animal and plant virologists through monthly group meetings in College Park, an annual retreat and graduate coursework in virology and immunology.

§ 1 · Specialized training

Two research tracks

The program concentrates its training on two central areas of modern virology.

I.
Track I

Host-virus interactions

How viruses hijack host cells, how cells fight back with innate immunity, and how viruses evade those defenses. Studying these interactions reveals how cells handle gene expression, RNA biology and membrane trafficking.

  • Virus-cell biology
  • Innate immunity
  • Immune evasion
Read about the track →
II.
Track II

Viral immunology & vaccine design

Engineered antigens for broad antibody and T cell responses, and mucosal vaccine platforms against HIV, influenza, RSV, SARS-CoV-2 and herpesviruses.

  • Antigen design
  • Mucosal vaccines
  • Antibodies
